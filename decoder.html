Signal decoder

The meter bars on this site are not random. Each one carries a message in Morse. Here is how to read it.

How the bars encode letters

Each column belongs to one of three height bands. Judge it by how far up the column reaches, or count the lit segments if you want to be exact:

What you seeLit segmentsMeans
A tall column, well into the top third11 to 15dash —
A middling column, around a third up4 to 7dot •
A stub at the base1end of a letter

One thing will stop any of this working. The radio on the front page has a control that unlocks the bars so they run the full height of the column with the music. That looks better and destroys the message, which is the trade. If the columns seem to reach the top constantly and nothing decodes, that is why: lock them again.

The bars bounce, but each one stays inside its own band. A dot at its highest is still four segments short of a dash at its lowest, and the rows between the bands never light up, so the reading does not change as they move. Stubs hold still.

Read left to right. Two stubs in a row mean a space between words. Every divider spells the name of the section below it, and the big animated bars in the header spell something else.

Worked example

The first divider on the front page reads:

• — | — • • • | — — — | • • — | —

Split at the stubs: .- -... --- ..- -, which is A, B, O, U, T.

Decode as you read

Tap a button for each column you see. Keyboard works too: full stop for a dot, hyphen for a dash, space for a letter break, backspace to undo.
